## Dual Power

 - The book (and Lenin et al) describe the situation between Feb and October as 'Dual Power'. What does that mean?

 - Features of dual power? Inherently unstable. Inherently temporary. Zero-sum game - one side or the other must win

 - > "The Provisional Government does not possess any real power, and its directives are carried out only to the extent that it is permitted by the Soviet of Workers’ and Soldiers’ Deputies, which enjoys all the essential elements of real power since the troops, the railroads, the post and telegraph are all in its hands. One can say flatly that the Provisional Government exists only so long as it is permitted by the Soviet."

## October and the path to workers power

Struggle ebbs and flows, the working class advance and retreat, but soon the Bolsheviks win leadership of the class.

- We've abstracted away a LOT of the ins and outs - so check again for any questions or things people want to talk about so we can make time to return to them in 10 mins.

- By October the majority of the working class and the peasants were convinced the only way to achieve demands (land, bread, peace) was through soviets taking power and overthrowing provisional government. Dual power resolved in favour of working class.

  - Feb Rev involved mass rallies for weeks, mutinies, riots, the burning of police stations and wide-spread factory occupations. October Rev seems almost quaint by comparison - no barricades in most cities, no desperate street-by-street fighting for control. Some historians argue this is evidence of October being a conspiracy - a minority usurping power in the name of the Bolshevik party. Are they right?

  - Why was the October revolution so (comparatively) peaceful? (Soviets already de-facto workers government, already running everything)
